# Discovery Brief Format

The Discovery Brief is the contract between `fsd-discovery` (which reads sources, conducts elicitation, and identifies stakeholders) and `fsd-architect` (which writes the actual FSD).

It is the BA equivalent of an engineer's context dossier — but with stakeholder analysis, business goal framing, and BABOK-grounded discovery output that an engineer-only workflow would skip.

The brief is written to `discovery/<project_slug>_discovery_brief.md`.

---

## Template

Every section below appears in every brief, in this order, even if empty (use "None identified.").

```markdown
# Discovery Brief — <Project Name>

**Generated by:** fsd-discovery
**Generated at:** <ISO 8601 timestamp>
**FSD scope:** <one sentence: what FSD this brief supports — e.g., "Phase 2 release of Customer Onboarding System">
**Discovery confidence:** High | Medium | Low — with one-sentence rationale

---

## 1. Source materials consumed

| # | Source | Type | Size | Read in full | Has diagrams |
|---|--------|------|------|--------------|--------------|
| 1 | /path/to/business-case.pdf | PDF | 12 pages | Yes | Yes (2) |
| 2 | Interview notes — COO (S-01) | transcript | ~40 turns | Yes | No |
| 3 | Existing process documentation | docx | 24 pages | Yes | Yes (BPMN, 3) |
| 4 | Regulatory citation: GDPR Art. 17 | reference | — | Yes | No |

## 2. Business problem statement

2–4 paragraphs synthesizing the problem in BUSINESS terms (not solution terms). Cover:

- What is the business currently failing to do well?
- What is the measurable impact (revenue, cost, risk, customer satisfaction)?
- Why now? (Regulatory deadline? Competitive pressure? Cost escalation?)
- What changes if we do nothing?

This is the language that goes into FSD §2.1 (with light editing).

---

## 3. Business goals & success metrics

Each goal: unique ID, statement, measurable metric, current baseline, target value, accountable owner.

```markdown
| ID | Business Goal | Success Metric | Baseline | Target | Owner | Source |
|----|---------------|----------------|----------|--------|-------|--------|
| G-01 | <goal> | <metric> | <value> | <value> | <role> | <stakeholder/doc ref> |
```

Every FR in the final FSD must trace to at least one of these goals. If you can't link a candidate FR to a goal, either:
- You're missing a goal (add it here, with the stakeholder source)
- The FR isn't valuable (don't write it)

---

## 4. Stakeholder register

Per BABOK v3 stakeholder analysis. Each stakeholder gets an ID (S-NN) used throughout the FSD.

```markdown
| ID | Name / Role | RACI | Influence | Interest | Primary concerns | Interviewed? |
|----|-------------|------|-----------|----------|------------------|--------------|
| S-01 | <role>, <name if known> | A/R/C/I | H/M/L | H/M/L | <bullets> | Yes/No |
```

Categorize and check for completeness:
- **Direct users** of the system
- **Indirect users** (downstream consumers of outputs)
- **Approvers** (who can veto)
- **Sponsors** (who fund / champion)
- **Operations / support** (who run it after launch)
- **Compliance / legal**
- **Security**
- **External: customers**
- **External: regulators**
- **External: partners / vendors**

Fewer than 6 stakeholders identified = likely missing groups. Flag in §13.

---

## 5. Current state (as-is) — process and pain points

Describe the current operating reality.

### 5.1 As-is process flow

Either a BPMN diagram description (boxes-and-arrows in words), or a numbered process flow:

```
1. Customer fills paper application form
2. Branch officer reviews for completeness
3. Officer scans form and emails to central ops
4. Central ops keys data into Customer System
5. ...
```

Include cycle time (best-known estimate), handoffs, manual steps, system touchpoints.

### 5.2 Top pain points (with measurable impact where known)

```markdown
| ID | Pain point | Frequency | Impact | Source |
|----|-----------|-----------|--------|--------|
| P-01 | Manual data re-entry | Every application | 12 min/case × 200/day = 40 hrs/day | S-03 interview |
| P-02 | Document loss in handoff | ~3% of applications | Compliance risk | Support ticket log |
```

Each pain point becomes a candidate driver for one or more FRs.

### 5.3 Current systems involved

| System | Owner | Role in current process | Sunset planned? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legacy CRM | IT | Customer record of truth | No |
| Email | IT | Document handoff | No |

---

## 6. Future state (to-be) — target operating model

The target post-implementation. Describe the experience and process, NOT the technology.

### 6.1 To-be process flow

Same format as 5.1 but for the future. Highlight what changed vs as-is.

### 6.2 To-be system context

What new system / capability sits where in the ecosystem.

### 6.3 Key changes vs current state

```markdown
| Capability | As-is | To-be | Change required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Application intake | Paper + manual entry | Digital self-service | New web/mobile UI |
| Document verification | Manual review | Automated + exception review | New verification capability |
```

---

## 7. High-level capability map

Hierarchical decomposition of the to-be system. NOT requirements yet — capabilities. Two or three levels deep.

```
<Project capability>
├── <Capability 1>
│   ├── <Sub-capability 1.1>
│   └── <Sub-capability 1.2>
├── <Capability 2>
│   ├── <Sub-capability 2.1>
│   └── <Sub-capability 2.2>
└── <Capability 3>
```

This becomes FSD §3.2 (and structures §5).

## 8. Constraints

Things that constrain the solution and aren't themselves requirements.

### 8.1 Regulatory

| ID | Regulation | Specific article/clause | Effective | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-REG-01 | GDPR | Art. 17 (Right to Erasure) | In force | Compliance |
| C-REG-02 | PCI-DSS 4.0 | Req. 3.4 (PAN protection) | In force | Security |

### 8.2 Technical / architectural

```
- Must integrate with existing identity provider (Okta)
- Data residency: EU only
- No SaaS for customer PII (on-prem mandated)
- Frontend must be web + mobile (no native apps in this release)
```

### 8.3 Organizational / budget / timeline

```
- Budget cap: $1.2M
- Hard deadline: Q3 release (regulatory)
- Cannot add headcount; must use existing teams
- Vendor X already chosen for document verification (locked in)
```

## 9. Assumptions & dependencies

### Assumptions

Things believed true but not verified. Each is a risk if wrong.

```markdown
| ID | Assumption | Confidence | Risk if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-01 | Vendor X API will be available by April 2026 | Medium | Build mock; renegotiate |
| A-02 | Existing customer data is clean enough for migration | Low | Add data cleanup epic |
```

### Dependencies

External requirements for this project to succeed.

```markdown
| ID | Dependency | Owner | Needed by | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | New identity provider integration | IT Platform team | 2026-03-15 | In planning |
```

## 11. Glossary — domain vocabulary

Every domain-specific term that will be used in the FSD. Two columns: term, definition (verbatim from sources where possible; cited otherwise).

```markdown
| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| KYC | Know Your Customer — regulatory process to verify identity | Compliance handbook §3.2 |
| Onboarding cycle | Time from application submission to active account | S-01 interview |
```

## 12. Source conflicts

Places where two sources disagree. Don't silently pick a winner.

```markdown
| ID | Conflict | Source A | Source B | Resolution needed from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| X-01 | Retention period | Compliance: 7 years | Ops: "as long as needed" | Compliance (authoritative) |
```

If no conflicts, write "None identified."

---

## 13. Open questions requiring stakeholder input

Critical questions that the BA cannot resolve from sources alone. **Each must be answered before fsd-architect can produce a complete FSD.**

```markdown
| ID | Question | Owner | Needed by | Why critical |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q-01 | Confirm retention period: 7 years or longer? | Compliance | Before FSD §7.2 written | Drives data architecture |
| Q-02 | Will mobile app be in Phase 2 scope? | Product owner | Before FSD §3 written | Affects UI/NFR-USABILITY |
| Q-03 | Is on-prem mandate firm or preference? | Architecture board | Before FSD §3.5 | Affects entire deployment model |
```

Maximum 8 critical questions. If you have more, you haven't synthesized — go back and identify the highest-leverage questions.

**The user MUST answer these (by editing this brief) before invoking `fsd-architect`.** The architect agent's prompt will refuse to write an FSD with open critical questions.

---

## 14. Discovery confidence assessment

One paragraph. Stating, honestly:

- What you read and understood well
- What sources had gaps you could not fill
- Where you inferred vs where you had explicit statements
- The overall confidence: High (sources comprehensive, gaps documented) / Medium (substantial coverage, some critical gaps) / Low (sparse sources, many inferences)

If Low, recommend additional discovery sessions before FSD writing.

## 15. Recommended FSD structure (preview)

Based on the discovery above, the architect agent should produce:

- **Primary FSD:** following the ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 structure
- **Section emphasis:** which sections will be content-heavy
  - e.g., "§5 (functional requirements) will have ~80 FRs across 6 capabilities"
  - e.g., "§6 (NFRs) needs special attention to compliance category given GDPR scope"
- **Companion documents needed:**
  - [ ] Use Case Catalog (if more than 15 use cases — split into separate doc)
  - [ ] RTM (always; produced by fsd-traceability after FSD is written)
  - [ ] Test Plan (separate doc; references FRs by ID)
- **Estimated FSD length:** ~N pages / ~M FRs

## How to fill each section — rules of thumb

| Section | Length | Style | Bad sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sources consumed | Table only | One row per source | "Various sources" anywhere |
| Business problem | 2–4 paragraphs | Business language; cite stakeholders | Solution language ("we will build...") |
| Goals & metrics | Table with 3–8 rows | Measurable metrics with baselines | Goals like "improve customer experience" with no metric |
| Stakeholder register | Table with 6–15 rows | Cover all category buckets | Fewer than 6 = missed groups |
| As-is process | Numbered flow or BPMN description | Cycle time, handoffs explicit | Solution language ("the new system will...") |
| Pain points | Table; measurable where possible | Frequency × Impact quantified | "Pain points exist" without specifics |
| To-be process | Mirror as-is structure | Highlight changes | Identical to as-is (no improvement clear) |
| Capability map | 2–3 levels, ~10–30 leaves | Capabilities, not requirements | FR-like leaves ("system shall...") |
| Constraints | Three subsections, all populated | Regulatory: full citations | Generic compliance ("we follow GDPR") |
| Assumptions | Each labeled with confidence | A-NN IDs | No confidence assigned |
| Dependencies | Each with owner and date | D-NN IDs | Owners as "team" not named role |
| Glossary | Table | Source cited | Definitions made up |
| Conflicts | Table | Both sides shown | Silently picked a winner |
| Open questions | ≤8 critical questions | Q-NN IDs with "needed by" | More than 8 (not prioritized) |
| Confidence assessment | One paragraph | Honest | Always "High" |

## Why this brief is BA-shaped, not engineer-shaped

The engineer-side context dossier (in the `spec-authoring` skill from this package's sibling, `opencode-spec-architect`) emphasizes:
- Source contents, decisions, gaps, glossary
- What the system is

The BA-side discovery brief above emphasizes:
- Stakeholders, business problem, goals, current pain, future vision
- Why the system exists, who needs it, what success looks like

Both are valid; they answer different questions. A high-quality FSD is built from the BA brief. A high-quality implementation plan is built from the engineering dossier. Same project, two artifacts, two starting points.

For projects that need both (regulated industries usually do), produce both: discovery brief first → FSD → engineering dossier → implementation plan.
