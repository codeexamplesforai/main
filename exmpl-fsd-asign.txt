1. How complexity is identified

  Complexity is not computed from the alert's content (amount, geography,
  transaction pattern, etc.). It's a governed static attribute on the taxonomy:

  - Every (Product × Risk-typology) pair carries a label: Standard or Complex
  (just two values in v1).
  - That label is set and maintained by the AML SME (S-05) / Head of Team (S-03)
  — not the engine (BR-02, FR-402).
  - So "is this complex?" is answered by a lookup, not a calculation.

  ▎ ⚠️  This is one of your [CONFIRM] items (A-09 / D-03). The mechanism is
  ▎ locked, but the actual classification of each pair is provisional and needs
  ▎ the AML SME to populate it. Also note v1 is deliberately binary — there's no
  ▎ "medium" tier and no content-based scoring. If the business actually wants
  ▎ complexity inferred from alert attributes, that's a different design and
  ▎ should be raised now.

  The mapping from complexity → required expertise (BR-02, the DMN-style
  decision table):

  ┌──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬───────────────────┐
  │   Complexity of (Product,    │    Min. required     │  Eligible levels  │
  │          Typology)           │      expertise       │                   │
  ├──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼───────────────────┤
  │ Standard                     │ Certified            │ Certified,        │
  │                              │                      │ Champion          │
  ├──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼───────────────────┤
  │ Complex                      │ Champion             │ Champion only     │
  └──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴───────────────────┘

  Two hard rules sit on top: Trainees are never auto-assigned (BR-02), and the
  engine never silently downgrades the requirement (BR-03 / FR-407) — a Complex
  alert with no Champion free goes to a queue, it does not fall back to a
  Certified analyst.

  2. The assignment algorithm (the pipeline)

  For each raised alert, the engine runs these steps in order (FR-401→FR-408):

  1. Validate the Alert Object has Product, Risk typology, details,
  Expected-close timeline (FR-401). Missing → reject.
  2. Derive required expertise by looking up the (Product, Typology) complexity
  (FR-402).
  3. Eligibility match — analysts certified for that Product and Typology,
  at-or-above the required level (FR-403, BR-01/BR-06; higher level satisfies
  lower).
  4. Availability filter — drop anyone unavailable across the Expected-close
  window (FR-404).
  5. Deterministic ranking (FR-405, BR-05) — a lexicographic tie-break (not a
  weighted score). Each step only breaks ties left by the previous one:
    a. Availability / remaining capacity across the window
    b. Lowest backlog
    c. Highest efficiency rate
    d. Earliest projected completion within the timeline
    e. Lowest stable analyst ID (guarantees exactly one winner, always)
  6. Assign + record an explainable, immutable decision (FR-406) — captures the
  eligibility basis and the ranking that picked them.
  7. Fallback ladder if nobody eligible-and-available: queue as
  NO_ELIGIBLE_ANALYST → Team Lead (FR-407) → escalate to Head of Team after 30
  min (FR-408).

  3. Worked example

  Incoming alert: Product = Wire Transfer, Typology = Trade-Based Money
  Laundering (TBML), Expected-close = 48h.

  Step 2 — complexity lookup: the taxonomy has (Wire Transfer, TBML) = Complex →
  required expertise = Champion (FR-402).

  Analyst pool:

  ┌─────────┬──────────────┬───────────┬─────────────┬─────────┬────────────┐
  │         │  Certified   │           │ Available   │         │            │
  │ Analyst │ for (Wire,   │   Level   │  next 48h?  │ Backlog │ Efficiency │
  │         │    TBML)?    │           │             │         │            │
  ├─────────┼──────────────┼───────────┼─────────────┼─────────┼────────────┤
  │ A-17    │ ✅           │ Champion  │ ✅          │ 12      │ 0.80       │
  ├─────────┼──────────────┼───────────┼─────────────┼─────────┼────────────┤
  │ A-09    │ ✅           │ Champion  │ ✅          │ 12      │ 0.92       │
  ├─────────┼──────────────┼───────────┼─────────────┼─────────┼────────────┤
  │ A-23    │ ✅           │ Certified │ ✅          │ 3       │ 0.95       │
  ├─────────┼──────────────┼───────────┼─────────────┼─────────┼────────────┤
  │ A-31    │ ✅           │ Champion  │ ❌ (on      │ 1       │ 0.99       │
  │         │              │           │ leave)      │         │            │
  └─────────┴──────────────┴───────────┴─────────────┴─────────┴────────────┘

  Step 3 — eligibility match (Champion only): A-17 and A-09 qualify. A-23 is
  dropped (only Certified — and critically, the engine will not downgrade to
  give it to her even though she has the lowest backlog and highest efficiency).
  A-31 qualifies on level.

  Step 4 — availability filter: A-31 removed (on leave across the window).
  Survivors: A-17, A-09.

  Step 5 — lexicographic ranking:
  - Step 1 (availability/capacity): both available — tie.
  - Step 2 (backlog): both 12 — tie.
  - Step 3 (efficiency): A-09 = 0.92 > A-17 = 0.80 → A-09 wins.

  Step 6 — assign to A-09, and write the decision record: "Required Champion
  (Wire×TBML = Complex); eligible {A-17, A-09} after availability; selected A-09
  — tie on capacity & backlog broken by efficiency 0.92 vs 0.80."

  Counterfactual — the fallback path: if A-17, A-09, and A-31 had all been
  unavailable, the only remaining certified person is A-23 (Certified, not
  Champion). Because the typology is Complex, the engine does not assign to her.
  The alert goes to the Unassigned (Pending) queue → Team Lead, who may
  manually override to A-23 — but only with a captured justification, logged as
  an exception visible to the Head of Team (BR-03 / FR-502). After 30 min
  unassigned, it escalates to the Head of Team (FR-408).

  ---
  The key design decisions to validate with your stakeholders:
  1. Complexity is a manual per-pair label, not inferred from alert data —
  confirm that's the intent (and get the AML SME to actually classify the
  pairs).
  2. Ranking is strict lexicographic, so a single factor (e.g., efficiency)
  fully decides once earlier factors tie — confirm the business doesn't prefer a
  weighted model where backlog and efficiency trade off (that's flagged
  [CONFIRM] A-11).
  3. No silent downgrade means Complex alerts can sit pending rather than go to
  a lower-tier analyst — confirm that's the desired risk posture (it protects
  quality but can delay routing).
